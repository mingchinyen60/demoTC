Solving the Deep-Sea Timing Crisis in
Ocean Bottom Nodes (OBN)

How TimeCubic's <60 mW ultra-low power disciplined oscillators solve the fundamental trade-off between seismic image resolution and seabed deployment longevity.

The Core Engineering Bottleneck

Zero GPS at 3,000 Meters Depth.
Every Millisecond of Clock Drift Is Positional Error.

In marine seismic exploration, thousands of autonomous Ocean Bottom Nodes (OBN) are deployed to the seabed for 60 to 180+ days. Because high-frequency electromagnetic radio signals (GPS/GNSS) cannot penetrate seawater, every node operates in blind holdover.

Seismic wave speed in rock and water means timing drift directly corrupts the spatial reconstruction of 3D/4D seismic imaging ($\Delta x = v \times \Delta t$). Conventional OCXOs consume 300 mW to 1.5 W—demanding massive lithium battery packs that bloat node dimensions, increase ROV deployment cycle times, and escalate vessel operational costs.

01

Drastic Battery Payload Reduction

Operating at under 60 mW, TimeCubic's Q50/Q100 modules slash timing power draw by over 70%. This enables seismic equipment OEMs to shrink node enclosure volume, lower handling weight on vessel decks, and deploy 2× more nodes per ROV basket.

02

Precision 1PPS Pre/Post Discipline

Prior to seabed deployment on the vessel deck, Q100 synchronizes with GNSS 1PPS down to 1 ns RMS phase accuracy and logs internal time-of-day (ToD) via RS232. Upon node retrieval, post-mission time drift is measured and verified for sub-millisecond data correction.

03

Deep-Sea Thermal Stability & Aging

Seabed water temperatures can drop to 2°C to 4°C with intense hydrostatic pressures. TimeCubic’s proprietary thermal isolation chambers and aging compensation algorithms guarantee holdover stability of < ±1 ms across 60 days.

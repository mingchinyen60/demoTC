1. The Mathematical Physics: Phase Noise to Aperture Jitter Transformation

In high-speed data acquisition, every analog-to-digital conversion occurs at the instant of a clock transition edge. If the sampling clock suffers from phase jitter $\Delta t$, the input voltage $V_{\text{in}}(t)$ is sampled at an uncertain time $t + \Delta t$, generating an instantaneous voltage error:

// 1. Sampling Voltage Error vs. Slew Rate:
ΔV = (dVin / dt) × Δtjitter = 2π fin Vpeak cos(2π fin t) × Δt
Because input slew rate scales linearly with analog input frequency fin, voltage sampling error increases dramatically as RF input frequencies rise into the multi-gigahertz realm.
// 2. Single-Sideband Phase Noise to RMS Jitter Integral:
σt = [ 1 / (2π f0) ] × √[ 2 × ∫f1f2 10ℒ(f)/10 df ]  [Seconds RMS]
Where ℒ(f) is single-sideband phase noise in dBc/Hz, integrated from close-in offsets (e.g., 12 kHz) out to the Nyquist bandwidth (e.g., 20 MHz or 100 MHz).
// 3. Ultimate Jitter-Limited ADC SNR Ceiling & ENOB:
SNRjitter = -20 × log10 ( 2π • fin • σt,total )  |  ENOB = (SNRjitter - 1.76) / 6.02
Where σt,total = √[ σclock2 + σADC,aperture2 ]. If clock jitter σclock exceeds internal ADC aperture jitter (~60-90 fs), the master clock completely dominates system noise and erodes usable converter bits!

2. Reciprocal Mixing: How Phase Noise Skirts Blind Weak Signals

In modern electronic warfare (EW) receivers, radar countermeasure systems, and congested 5G cellular bands, strong interfering signals (blockers) frequently sit only a few megahertz away from weak target signals of interest.

When an ADC samples an analog spectrum containing a high-power adjacent blocker ($P_{\text{blocker}}$), the blocker convolves with the clock's phase noise sidebands $\mathcal{L}(\Delta f)$ in the frequency domain. This phenomenon—known as reciprocal mixing—transfers the clock's phase noise power directly into the intermediate frequency or digital baseband of the weak desired signal:

Reciprocal Mixing Noise Power Density (NRM):
NRM = Pblocker + ℒ(Δf) + 10 log10(BWchannel)  [dBm]
If a +10 dBm blocker arrives at an offset Δf = 100 kHz, and the reference oscillator's phase noise at 100 kHz is poor (-130 dBc/Hz), the resulting noise power in a 1 MHz channel is -60 dBm—completely burying a -100 dBm weak radar echo!
